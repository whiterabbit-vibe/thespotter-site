04Tasks & Notes

A board with your real files on the cards.

Tasks is a Kanban board, with columns you name. The difference: each card holds the actual files. Put a video on a card and it plays right there. Copy the card's link into Calendar, Slack or Mail, and one click brings you back to that card with the file ready.

  • Columns you name

    Start Simple (To do, Doing, Waiting, Done), Client work or Creator, then rename, add and move columns. Projects are colored chips. See it as a Board, a List or your Week.

  • Files that show themselves

    Videos play on the card, PDFs scroll, a folder shows its newest file. When a newer version sits beside a file (cut_v4 next to cut_v3), the card asks if you want it.

  • One link opens the exact card

    Copy Link on a card, a file or a note, and paste it anywhere. Add to Calendar puts the link in the event. A link holds random letters, never a file name, and it only opens; it never changes anything.

  • Files that move are found again

    Rename or move a file and the card still finds it. In the Trash, or on a drive that isn't plugged in? The card says so, calmly.

  • Many ways in

    Add to Task… on any file, drag from Finder, or ask Spot: “make a task to edit IMG_6012.MOV due Friday”. Spot may suggest up to three cards a day from your calendar and the dates in your documents, each with a Why. It never adds one by itself.

07Private

Kept on your Mac. Tested in the open.

Spotter reads your files on your Mac and keeps what it learns there. No account, no analytics, and nothing leaves your Mac without a click.

  • Your files never leave your Mac

    Search, text in images, transcripts of recordings and answers all run on your Mac, with Apple's on-device model and the small open models you choose to download once.

  • The index is encrypted

    What Spotter reads from your files, your tasks and notes, the small pictures in Activity, and your recent searches are stored with AES-256 encryption. The key lives in your Keychain. The list of folders it reads or never reads, and your settings, stay unencrypted on purpose, so Spotter still knows what never to read if the key is ever lost.

  • No account, no analytics

    The app sends nothing about you or your files. Once a day it checks for an update, sending its version number. Each lookup on Wikipedia or Apple Maps asks first, carries only your question's words, and is listed in the app before it's sent.

  • Nothing changes until you say so

    Moves, renames and clean-ups show a plan first. Nothing is deleted for good: it goes to the Trash, and one Undo puts everything back.

  • Faces only if you ask

    Recognizing people is off until you turn it on. It runs on your Mac, and you can erase the faces whenever you like.

  • You choose what it reads

    Pick the folders in Settings, and add any folder to Never read. A folder holding a .nospotter file is always skipped. The status line shows what Spotter has read, pass by pass.

Questions

How is this different from Spotlight?

Spotlight matches the words you type against file names and some text. Spotter reads what's inside your files, including text in screenshots and scans and what's said in recordings, and understands related words, dates and places. "Insurance for the cat" finds a policy that says "Luna" and "vet visits". With the meaning model, it also finds a file when none of your words are in it. It can also answer questions with the page the answer came from, and show the files around a meeting.

Does anything get uploaded?

No. Reading, searching and answering all happen on your Mac. The app sends a daily update check carrying its version number (you can turn it off), and downloads a new version from GitHub when you install one. It downloads an open model only when you choose it: the meaning model (639 MB) and the picture model (199 MB) when you press Continue in setup with smart search ticked, or later in Settings → Intelligence; written answers in setup too, on a Mac without Apple Intelligence (ticked, and nothing downloads until you press Continue); the second look and photo descriptions only from Settings. Each lookup of a general question on Wikipedia or Apple Maps asks you first, every time, and sends just the words of the question, never a name that appears in your files. Each of these requests is listed on Spotter's Privacy page before it's sent, and like any request from any app, each carries your Mac's internet (IP) address. The Places map is Apple's, and loads its map imagery from Apple as you move around it.

What is the Tasks board?

A Kanban board inside Spotter, in the sidebar under Work. Cards sit in columns you name (it starts with To do, Doing, Waiting and Done, or the Client work or Creator presets), and each card can hold files, a due day, a checklist and notes. Drag a file onto a card, or choose Add to Task… on any file. Open a card and its files show themselves: videos play, PDFs scroll. Move or rename a file and the card still finds it. Your tasks are kept in Spotter's encrypted index, on your Mac.

How does a link open the exact card?

Copy Link on a card, a file or a note gives you a link that starts with spotter://. Paste it into Calendar, Slack, Mail or anywhere else; clicking it opens Spotter at that card, file or note. The link is made of random letters, so it never shows a file name, a title or a folder. A link only opens things; it never changes anything. On a Mac that doesn't have that card, Spotter says the link points to something on another Mac.

How do I copy the text in a picture, or on my screen?

Right-click a picture, a screenshot or a PDF and choose Copy Text from Image (⌃⌘C). For anything on your screen, choose Copy Text from Screen (⌃⇧⌘T, or Copy Text in the menu bar panel or the quick panel) and drag over the part you want. A notice says “Text copied” over whatever app you're in. The words are read on your Mac with Apple's Vision. The first time you copy from the screen, macOS asks you to allow Screen Recording for Spotter.

What does Spot do in the menu bar?

Click Spot for a small panel: search, how your Mac is doing in one line, the apps using the most memory with Quit and Force Quit (each asks first; Finder and Spotter are never offered), quick tools (Copy Text, Note, Downloads, Tidy, Space) and your recent documents. Spot turns orange and frowns while the disk is nearly full, memory is tight or the Mac is hot, and smiles again when that passes. Right-click it for more, including Pause Reading Files.

Do I need Apple Intelligence?

Search works without it, including search by meaning and photos by what's in them, which use their own small models. Answers, summaries and related words use Apple's on-device model, so they need a Mac with Apple Intelligence turned on; without it, the optional Gemma model can write the answers.

What happens after the trial?

Spotter pauses until you enter a license key. Nothing is deleted: your index, tasks, notes and settings wait on your Mac, and everything picks up where it left off.

Can it change or delete my files?

Only when you ask. Every move, rename or clean-up shows a plan you approve first. Nothing is deleted for good: files go to the Trash, and one Undo reverses it.

What are the keys?

⇧⌥Space opens quick search from any app (you can choose another shortcut). Return opens the selected file or app, ⌘Return shows it in Spotter, ⌥Return shows it in Finder, and ⌘Y lists your last downloads so you can drag one into any app. ⌃⌥Space opens Ask Spot from any app, and ⌃⌥N adds a task from any app (Quick Entry). Setup turns both on; switch either off in setup or in Settings → About.

Can I just say what I'm looking for?

Yes. Click the microphone in the box and talk: "the hotel receipt from Big Sur". Your words are turned into text on your Mac, appear in the box as you speak, and the search runs when you pause. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. The first time, macOS may download Apple's speech model for your language; that download is listed in Privacy too.

What can Ask Spot do?

It answers and it acts. Ask about a file (“summarize the lease”, “what deadlines do I have in December”), about everything you keep (“how many photos do I have”, “what did I work on last week”), about your Mac (free space, memory, battery, how long it's been on, which apps take the most space) or about where you've been (“when did I visit San Francisco”). Ask it to change a Mac setting and it opens the right page of System Settings and says where the switch is. Or give it a job: “put my 2025 invoices in one folder, zip it and email it to my accountant”, “make a task to edit the Tahoe video due Friday”, “every Friday at 5, make my expense report”. For a job, Spot shows what will change and waits for your yes; email is left as a draft for you to send; and everything it does can be undone.

Is Ask Spot finished?

No, it's in Beta, and it says so. It answers what it can check: what a document says, with the file and page; dates and deadlines in your documents; spending at a store or on a kind of thing, with a line saying how it read your question; and questions about your Mac. It checks each name and number against your files and leaves out what it can't find. It can't yet tell paid from unpaid invoices, and says so. Open the file to be sure.

What if my files don't have the answer?

Spot says so (“No file mentions Alex Moreno”), and for a general question it offers to look it up on Wikipedia or Apple Maps. It asks every time, unless you turn on Always allow lookups in Settings → Privacy. Only the words of your question leave your Mac, never a file name, anything from a file, or a name that appears in your files. Each lookup is listed in Privacy.

What does Tidy remove, and can I get it back?

Check my Mac suggests forgotten downloads, old installers, duplicates, look-alike photos, big old videos, apps you no longer use, and the caches, logs, half-finished downloads and leftovers apps leave behind. The safe ones start ticked; you choose what goes. For duplicates it keeps the original, not the copy named “copy” or “(1)”. It never touches a folder under version control. Everything goes to the Trash, and one Undo (on the receipt or in Activity) brings it back.

Can it find a photo by what's in it?

Yes. Spotter already reads the text in photos and screenshots and where photos were taken ("photos from San Francisco"), and which camera took them ("videos from my iPhone"). Photos are dated by the camera, not by the day they were copied. With the picture model (199 MB, part of smart search, downloaded only when you choose it in setup or Settings → Intelligence), it also matches photos and videos to what they show, so "the dog on the sofa" or "sunset over the sea" finds the picture. This works in English and some European languages. All of it runs on your Mac; nothing is sent anywhere.

How do I keep a folder private?

Leave it out in Settings → Folders, add it to Never read there, or put a file named .nospotter inside it. Spotter never reads a folder that has one.

Does Spotter recognize faces?

Only if you turn it on. In People you give Spot a few photos of someone and say who they are; after that “photos of Maria” or “my dad's passport” works. Recognition runs on your Mac and nothing about a face is sent anywhere. You can mark someone's photos Handle with Care, and erase the faces whenever you like.

How many Macs can I use it on?

One license is for one person, on up to two of your Macs (say, an iMac at work and a MacBook at home). Moving to a new Mac? In Settings → License choose Remove Key from This Mac, then enter the key on the new one.
